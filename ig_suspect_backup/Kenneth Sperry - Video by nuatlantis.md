## Description

This used to be our world. 

At the turn of the 20th century, the world briefly glimpsed a different future. 

Nikola Tesla, working as chief engineer at Westinghouse Electric, helped bring alternating current to the great World’s Fairs. 

For the first time, millions saw entire cities illuminated at night, electricity not as utility, but as wonder. At the same time, Tesla was already moving beyond wires, experimenting with resonance, wireless transmission, and radiant energy.

Parallel to this, industrial power was consolidating. John D. Rockefeller controlled oil, Andrew Carnegie controlled steel, and J. P. Morgan initially funded Tesla’s Wardenclyffe Tower. 

When Morgan realized Tesla envisioned global communication, and potentially free, unmetered energy, funding was withdrawn. Tesla would later die penniless.

Before World War I began, electroculture, using atmospheric and telluric electricity to enhance plant growth, was researched and funded by multiple nations.

 Electrotherapy, using high-frequency and oscillatory devices, was mainstream medicine. 

Figures like Georges Lakhovsky explored cellular resonance; Royal Raymond Rife investigated frequency-based approaches to disease. 

But as petroleum-based fertilizers, pharmaceuticals, and industrial chemicals expanded — many linked to oil interests, funding for electrical and biological approaches quietly vanished. 

Lakhovsky died after being struck by a car; Rife was buried under lawsuits and professional ruin.

After World War I and World War II, the world emerged fully industrialized, centralized, and commercialized. 

What followed was a system built not for beauty or free thinkers, but for control.

The world we have today was manufactured to be this way to crush the human spirit and turn man into a machine.
